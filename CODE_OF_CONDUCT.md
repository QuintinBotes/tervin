# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be straight with people, and assume they are being straight with you.

## What that means here

**Say what you actually verified.** This project's whole premise is that a tool should
not claim more than it can do, and the same standard applies to the people working on it.
"I tested this" when you did not costs a reviewer more time than saying nothing would
have.

**Disagree about the work, not the person.** "This will deadlock because the guard
outlives the statement" is useful. "This is sloppy" is not, and it is not more true for
being blunter.

**Take a correction and move on.** Everyone here will be wrong about something; several
bugs in this codebase were mine, found by tests I wrote to prove the opposite. Being
wrong is cheap. Defending it is not.

**Ask rather than guess.** A question is faster than a wrong assumption, for both of us.

## Not acceptable

Harassment, personal attacks, discriminatory language, and unwelcome attention. Also:
deliberately wasting people's time: reports you have not checked, pull requests you have
not run, and arguments you know are not the real objection.

## Reporting

Email the maintainer, or open a
[private security advisory](https://github.com/QuintinBotes/tervin/security/advisories/new)
if a public issue would make things worse. Reports are handled privately.

The maintainer decides what to do about a report, and will say what was decided to the
person who raised it. That is a small project's honest answer: there is no committee,
and pretending otherwise would be exactly the kind of overclaiming this codebase exists
to avoid.
